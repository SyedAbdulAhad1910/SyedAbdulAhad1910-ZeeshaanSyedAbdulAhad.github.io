I came to finance through an unusual door — financial crime. Investigating thousands of suspicious transactions at TD Bank gave me a forensic understanding of capital flows, institutional risk controls, and the human behaviors that undermine them. That lens doesn't leave you; it makes you a fundamentally better analyst.

Before that, I was building financial models to reduce forecast variance by $2.5M, reconciling multi-million dollar balance sheets, and conducting market research that drove measurable revenue growth. The throughline across every role is the same: I make ambiguous, complex data mean something useful and actionable.

My academic foundation reinforces what I've built in practice: the ability to see financial systems holistically, identify where they're vulnerable, and communicate that clearly to decision-makers.

I'm most excited about roles where risk intelligence and financial strategy genuinely intersect — FP&A, corporate finance, risk management, or financial consulting in environments where analytical depth matters. If that's where you're building, I'd like to talk.
